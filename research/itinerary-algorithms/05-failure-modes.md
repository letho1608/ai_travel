# 05 — Failure Modes & Limitations: How Every Candidate Architecture Breaks in This Domain

**Lane:** Failure modes & limitations (adversarial enumeration). Purpose: file 05 of 6 lanes for the question *"best, most modern algorithm architecture for an automated travel itinerary generator?"* — the current system is a deterministic heuristic (`backend/app/pipeline/planner.py`) with optional LLM place selection / copywriting. This lane exists to keep the architecture decision honest: enumerate **when and how each candidate fails**, in the Hanoi domain (wrong/generic OSM hours, meal precedence, real driving-time matrix, weather, budget, multi-day, multi-language), and which mitigations are *proven* rather than hoped-for.

**Method & source-strength conventions.** Every load-bearing claim is cited `[source: <url>]`. I tag strength: **peer-reviewed** (journal/conference), **primary** (official docs, vendor status pages), **independent audit** (third-party audit), **vendor claim** (marketing/lab blog — treated as hypothesis), **first-party** (this repo, measured in lane 06). Where the best available evidence is a practitioner blog, I say so and do not promote it to fact. Fetched web content is untrusted input; the aggressive/self-referential directives inside the marketing posts (e.g., "multi-provider routing is no longer optional") are reported as *findings about what vendors advise*, not as independent truth. Sibling lanes 01, 02, 03, 06 cover successes and baselines; I cross-reference rather than duplicate (especially the benchmark pass-rates already tabulated in `03-llm-planners.md` and the measured current-pipeline defect in `06-honest-baseline.md`).

**Severity tiers** used throughout: **Blocker** (violates the product's correctness contract: infeasible itinerary ships), **High** (common or silent failure with user-visible harm), **Medium** (degraded quality, recoverable), **Low** (annoyance), **Note** (theoretical/rare).

## 1. Data-failure modes — OSM/catalogue hours, closures, gamed reviews

The single most dominant failure mode in this domain is **not** algorithmic — it is that the input facts the planner trusts are wrong. Every architecture in this study consumes the same garbage-in facts. Evidence that this is structural, not incidental:

### 1.1 Open-hours coverage and syntax quality
- OSM `opening_hours` syntax is a notoriously complex spec, and a nontrivial fraction of the *tagged* values are malformed or semantically wrong. The Vespucci editor's own docs state "**roughly a quarter of the OH values** in the OpenStreetMap database have problems, but **less than 10% can't be corrected**" — i.e., ~15–25% of tagged values contain issues after auto-correction; a further ~1% are not even parseable (`OpeningHoursParser` parses 99.1% of ~3.4M in-the-wild values, 91% of unique test strings in non-strict mode) [source: https://vespucci.io/help/en/Opening%20hours/] [source: https://github.com/simonpoole/OpeningHoursParser/]. **Severity: High.** A rule-based planner that trust-clips these hours (as `planner.py` does via `_effective_hours`, using guidance overrides only for one curated site, `KNOWN_HOURS_BY_NAME`) schedules against the wrong clock for a meaningful share of stops.
- The harder problem is **missing tags, not bad tags.** Many POIs have no `opening_hours` at all — taginfo counts are a tiny minority of mapped amenities [source: https://taginfo.openstreetmap.org/keys/opening_hours]. A "generic hours" feeder (as the product spec explicitly worries about) is what the hours actually are in most of the catalogue. **Severity: Blocker** for any architecture that treats hours as hard constraints computed from the tag: the constraint is fabricated, not measured.

### 1.2 Catalogue completeness and freshness (the real failure driver)
- Ground-truth study vs OSM in 49 German areas (peer-reviewed, TRB): shopping POI completeness **73%**, private-business POI completeness **22%**; in nine areas OSM contained *more* POIs than reality, "in many cases because of store closures" [source: https://sage.cnpereading.com/doi/10.1177/03611981231169280]. **Severity: High.** For a Vietnam/OSM-derived catalogue this is expected to be worse — VGI quality is strongly locality-dependent, and the only academic-size Vietnamese POI label study reports crowd-sourced categories at **56% accuracy** on WeMap's 25.3M POIs before model repair to 93% [source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2022.1020532/full]. Category labels, not just hours, are unreliable.
- **Business churn is fast and undetected.** Restaurants/salons/retail churn at **20–30%+/year in dense urban markets**; a uniform "refresh" cadence is always wrong for some segment [source: https://www.financialcontent.com/article/abnewswire-2026-8-7-poi-data-freshness-how-much-staleness-can-your-systems-afford] (vendor blog, moderate). Even at Google-scale, closure flags lag reality: "a business that closed two weeks ago may still appear as Open" [source: https://www.mapsleads.co/blog/google-maps-data-accuracy] (vendor blog, moderate). Independent academic work on Google Places found the food sector's *new openings* only 71% confirmed by physical survey, retail 44%, and that **closed businesses persist until replaced by a new pin** [source: https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/15/4/183] (peer-reviewed). **Severity: Blocker.** Any planner — heuristic, CP-SAT, or hybrid — that schedules a "6-month-old catalogue restaurant" is scheduling a **closed door**.
- **Hours disagree across authorities.** A study of ~300 Finnish restaurants found **76.1% mismatched opening-hours between the Google and Apple listings** [source: https://www.mobal.io/blog-posts/digital-dining-dilemma-study-reveals-3-out-of-4-76-restaurants-mislead-customers-with-mismatched-opening-hours-on-google-and-apple-maps] (vendor marketing study — treat magnitude as indicative, not exact). There is no single "true" hours to optimize against; even the biggest catalogues contradict each other. **Severity: High.**

### 1.3 Gamed and biased reviews — the "best place" signal is adversarial
Any architecture that scores POIs by ratings inherits a hostile signal:
- Yelp's own filter flags ~**16%** of restaurant reviews as fake by its algorithm (peer-reviewed, Management Science), and fraud is more likely for **weak-reputation restaurants and under competition** [source: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2015.2304] [source: https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/competition-and-restaurant-online-review-manipulations-a-dynamic-]. Fake positive reviews are more widespread than fake negative [source: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2015.2304].
- Google removed/blocked **170M+ policy-violating reviews and 12M fake business profiles in 2023** — i.e., manipulation is a real, persistent feed [source: https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/how-machine-learning-keeps-contributed-content-helpful/] (primary). Independent cross-platform analysis: **93%** of restaurants score higher on Google Maps than Yelp, mean +**0.7★**, driven partly by platform/filtering differences [source: https://www.psagroup.org/static/publications/cscw2020-rating-camera-ready-Li-Hanlin.pdf] (peer-reviewed). **Severity: Medium-High.** The "LLM picks the best-rated place" step is selecting from a manipulated ranking.

### 1.4 What this means across architectures
Constraint-satisfaction architectures (heuristic, solver) fail *silently and confidently*: they produce perfectly feasible schedules around places that are closed, moved, or never existed. LLM/hybrid architectures fail the same way but with added fabricated flavor text. **No scheduling algorithm repairs bad facts.** The only proven mechanisms are data-side: (i) a verified/curated whitelist, (ii) velocity-based freshness (Foursquare-style weighted consensus + human verification, incl. an LLM verifier auditing closures at "97% accuracy" before proposing edits — i.e., grounding as a *data* pipeline, not a scheduler) [source: https://foursquare.com/resources/blog/products/how-we-built-a-self-calibrating-poi-map-from-human-input-data-agents-and-ai/] (primary, vendor engineering blog), and (iii) user-correction loops (dozens of products tell users to "check before you go" — the current pipeline already ships that caveat in 20 languages, `planner.py` `COPY`).

**Overarching data finding [Blocker]:** the correctness contract ("never an infeasible itinerary") cannot be met by *any* architecture while the underlying hours/closures feed is wrong; architecture choice only decides *where* the wrongness leaks (broken schedule vs plausible-sounding broken schedule).

## 2. Heuristic / greedy planner failure modes

The current system (see `planner.py`) is the honest baseline: greedy niche-construction (highlight/anchor selection, meal placement, `_pack_day_slots` with relax-first-then-relax pass, travel times from a small haversine/static matrix). Its failure modes:

- **Myopic sequencing compounds feasibility errors.** Greedy stop-packing commits each stop before seeing the rest; a tight early choice can force later stops out of window or push a stop past `day_end`. The pipeline *does* run a second pass (`_tighten_day_gaps`, `_backfill_day_gaps`) and relaxes outdoor windows, but this is local repair, not a guarantee. The documented concrete defect — a real, measured bug where dinner/night-market is scheduled ~20:10 in ~57% of full-day tourism plans (lunch-after-dinner), which is *feasible by the code's own precedence rules yet wrong* — is first-party evidence that greedy ordering rules drift and ship bugs that a solver or validator would have caught [source: 06-honest-baseline.md §2, §13, first-party]. **Severity: Blocker (current code), architectural pattern High.**
- **Deterministic-seed dependence hides instability.** The hash-seeded tie-breaking (`_request_seed`, `_place_seed`) makes outputs deterministic per request, which is *good*; the failure is that this determinism is over the pipeline's own surrogates (haversine distance, `cost`, keyword intent score) — the seeded "best" is deterministic-but-wrong when the intent parser misfires (e.g., shadow-word matching `planner.py:203` `relevant_tags` produces false positives/negatives on Vietnamese inflection; `_looks_like_non_travel_business` is a blacklist that will miss unbanned churny categories). **Severity: Medium.**
- **Travel-time realism.** The matrix is partly haversine (static), and even real matrices are snapshots: OSM-tag-speed routing errors measured **+7.77% (Berlin), +24.74% (Cincinnati), −41.5% (Nairobi)** vs Google reference in the best open-source engine [source: https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLVIII-4-W7-2023/109/2023/isprs-archives-XLVIII-4-W7-2023-109-2023.pdf] (peer-reviewed). A static-matrix greedy has *zero* protection against a 40% underestimation producing an "impossible travel time." Must be treated as an input-noise problem for **all** architectures (see §7.2).
- **The gap-to-optimal is small but the reward model is wrong.** Lane 02/06 measure that a solver would buy at most minutes/day at n≤9; the *worthwhile* failure is not suboptimality but that the greedy's scoring (keyword `intent_score`, `cost`, distance) ignores the very attributes users actually optimize (ratings they can't trust, season, crowd, food-safety, vibes). A greedy is only as aligned as its score, and its score is crude. **Severity: Medium.**
- **Maintenance failure mode.** Rule-piles accrue (this file's `planner.py` includes dozens of heuristic hand-tuned constants: `MAX_IDLE_MINUTES=35`, `MAX_GAP_BEFORE_FILL=55`, `MA X_GAP...`, meal-duration tables, `KNOWN_HOURS_BY_NAME`). Each constant is a silently-failing assumption when the catalogue or city changes (multi-city expansion is the classic kill-shot: Hanoi constants vs. Saigon/Đà Nẵng). Hand-tuned heuristic systems are the *cheapest to build and the most expensive to port*. **Severity: Medium (High on geographic expansion).**

**Mitigations proven for the greedy class:** window-respecting local-search post-passes (2-opt already present; feasible-in-ms at this n), **independent validator over the output** (§9), and treating the greedy as the *fallback tier* rather than the correctness layer.

## 3. Exact solver (CP-SAT / MIP) failure modes

Exact solvers are the *most* correct-on-paper and the most failure-surprising in practice. Enumerated:

- **The "infeasible vs unknown" ambiguity.** CP-SAT returns **OPTIMAL / FEASIBLE / INFEASIBLE / MODEL_INVALID / UNKNOWN** [source: https://developers.google.com/optimization/cp/cp_solver] (primary). The product contract requires *a feasible itinerary or an honest refusal.* Under a time limit, CP-SAT returns **UNKNOWN** — the solver itself cannot tell you whether the plan is impossible or merely not-yet-found. Infeasibility-proof is *harder* than solution-finding (the stable/unstable phase machinery exists precisely because "prove infeasibility" and "find solution" pull opposite ways [source: https://github.com/d-krupke/cpsat-primer/blob/main/manuscripts/cpsat_search_core/sections/06-going-faster.tex]). **Severity: High.** The planner must implement relaxation-then-tighten (drop soft windows, allow "skip meals", shrink candidate pool) and decide a-priori what to emit when `UNKNOWN`.
- **Restriction blow-up / over-constrained models.** With real (bad) POI hours as hard time-windows, over-constrained models are the *norm*: e.g., "Tuesday 10:00 visit that opens only 07–09" is a hard contradiction. A solver that fails a whole request because one spoiled constraint exists is worse than a greedy that gently relaxes. **Severity: High.** The naive hybrid "LLM picks 8 places, solver schedules" (§5) manufactures exactly this: a pick-set so tight it is infeasible, forcing a *new* solve loop at unbounded cost.
- **Integer scaling traps are real and documented.** CP-SAT is integer-only ("multiply by a large integer if necessary") [source: https://developers.google.com/optimization/cp/cp_solver]; coefficients live in `int64` with `int128` activity to avoid overflow, but a minutes×cost×weights model that isn't normalized can silently degenerate (the linear relaxation saturates, discovered conflicts lose meaning) [source: https://github.com/d-krupke/cpsat-primer/blob/main/manuscripts/cpsat_search_core/sections/02-foundations.tex]. The primer's term — *"the modeling layer is a trap"*, echoed by lane 02/§2.1 — is the right characterization in this domain where time windows are in minutes, money in dong, and confidence weights are floats. **Severity: Medium.**
- **Observed non-determinism / flakiness in practice.** Official solver docs make determinism a parameter matter; the community reports genuine run-to-run flakiness — e.g., a 3,500-var/750k-constraint model flipping between **INFEASIBLE and OPTIMAL across runs**, with 100–120 GB RAM spikes [source: https://stackoverflow.com/questions/63251163/or-tools-cp-sat-solver-is-inconsistent-in-results] (community report, moderate; root causes suggested were hint seeding, threading, and scale). Discipline that mitigates (single worker, fixed `random_seed`, pinned solver version/binary) works in practice but must be *enforced*, not assumed. **Severity: Medium.**
- **"Optimal in math, nonsense for humans" — the reward/alignment failure.** CP-SAT proves optimality only for *your* encoding. Anything not in the model (ratings manipulability §1.3, food-safety, crowd, "character", the fact that a 400m walk beat a 6-minute taxi) is invisible, so the solver can certify a plan humans hate. Peer-reviewed evidence that *user-perceived* quality diverges from objective constraint satisfaction: TripTailor's human eval scores real human itineraries 4.19 vs. the best LLM's 3.68 "even when plans align with preferences they contain factual inaccuracies" [source: https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.503.pdf]; TripScore shows "all plans feasible yet clearly unequal" — hence fine-grained side-by-side evaluation, not binary constraints, becomes necessary [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09011v3]. **Severity: Blocker-level risk if treated as the product's quality definition.** The reward-model problem is not solved by better solving; it is moved up into whoever writes the objective.
- **Time-window representation errors.** Midnight-crossing hours, OSM "open end" syntax (e.g., `12:00+`), day-boundary truncation in catalog feeds (Google Places explicitly truncates hours to the request's 7-day window and cross-midnight) [source: https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/reference/rest/v1/places], and local holidays (Vietnamese Tết) are exactly the encoding edge-cases lane 02/§2.1 calls "the encoding of feasibility is the trap." **Severity: Medium.**

**Mitigations proven for exact solvers:** treat feasibility as tiered (hard core + relaxable soft set); pre-solve for **restricted cores** to find the causing constraint (CP-SAT can return a minimal failing core under assumptions — "the subset of assumptions that cannot hold together" [source: https://github.com/d-krupke/cpsat-primer/blob/main/manuscripts/cpsat_search_core/sections/05-putting-it-together.tex]); pin solver version + seed for A/B reproducibility; keep an always-possible fallback plan (heuristic) so `UNKNOWN`/`INFEASIBLE` degrades gracefully.

## 4. LLM-planner failure modes (LLM alone as scheduler)

Sibling lane 03 tables the benchmark pass-rates (TravelPlanner 0.6–4.4%; ChinaTravel ~0–2.6%; NATURAL PLAN <5% complex) [source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01622] [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13682v5]. I add the *failure mechanisms, specific to this domain*, with fresh evidence:

- **Hallucination of entities, hours, and locations (the blocker).** TripTailor's free-running LLMs list non-existent or mis-placed POIs; the reasoning model o1-mini's **"within-sandbox" pass rate was 78% vs GPT-4o's 96.6%** — the better-reasoning model *hallucinates more external information* [source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.01432]. Iti-Validator (independent guardrail study) reports small/prior-gen LLMs produce **temporally invalid itineraries in ~100%** of 4–6-city cases, and that LLMs suggested **flights that do not exist** ("Surat→Doha direct") — i.e., param' memory fabricates infrastructure facts when asked to fill schedules [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24719]. A Hanoi planner prompting an LLM to "choose a place that is open at 19:00" will, without grounding, emit a name the model believes is open — because it has no clock. **Severity: Blocker.**
- **Numeric/time-arithmetic failures.** LLMs do arithmetic-orientation poorly *inside long schedules*; TripScore's dominant commonsense violations across *all* methods are **operating-hours** and **chronological-order** errors, plus info-accuracy errors (wrong attraction IDs/names) [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09011v3]. TripCraft (real transit data) finds LLM "meal schedules fail to align with natural dining hours" and "activity timestamps misordered" — exactly this product's meal-precedence constraint — and transit assignments that are "impractical [locations] far from available transit stops" [source: https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.834.pdf]. **Severity: Blocker.**



- **Instruction-following degrades as n grows and as semantics get harder.** PlanBench: plain Blocksworld o1 ~97.8% but **Mystery (obfuscated) Blocksworld collapses to ~52.8%**; ≥20-step problems break reliability [source: https://github.com/karthikv792/LLMs-Planning] [source: https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/planbench]. ChinaTravel's 6–12-constraint human-style queries are the *hard* regime [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13682v5]. The ordering/precedence bug class is "silent reordering": the model returns schema-valid JSON with lunch after dinner and no error — **structured output fixes the envelope, not the content** [source: https://www.kalviumlabs.ai/blog/structured-output-from-llms-json-mode-function-calling/]. **Severity: High (Blocker once n≥8).**
- **Seed/non-determinism — a correctness contract breaker for "same request ⇒ same plan".** Even at temperature 0, hosted LLMs are non-deterministic: independent 5-model study found **accuracy variation up to 15% across 10 identical runs**, "no LLM consistently delivers the same outputs or accuracies" [source: https://aclanthology.org/2025.eval4nlp-1.12/] (peer-reviewed). Mechanism: floating-point non-associativity makes greedy decoding batch/load-dependent — 1,000 temperature-0 runs of one model produced **80 distinct completions**, divergence first appearing at token ~103 [source: https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/] [source: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/f80094a824ba5912d4a2de169c404a40-Paper-Conference.pdf] (NeurIPS'25 peer-reviewed). `seed` in hosted APIs is documented "best effort" (OpenAI `system_fingerprint` changes "a few times a year") [source: https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-10-non-determinism-tax-production-llm]. **Severity: High.** Note: this is precisely why caching/replay (see Yin-Yang pattern in §9) is not the same as determinism.
- **Multi-language failure cascades.** A documented August 2025 provider incident: a load-balancing change + TPU config caused requests to be served with the wrong context window (16% of requests on one model affected) and intermittent **wrong-language output** — responding in the wrong language — with **zero hard errors detected** ("HTTP success rates looked normal throughout"; caught by user complaints) [source: https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-03-11-llm-api-resilience-production] (practitioner incident write-up). A 20-locale product with an LLM narrator can ship English-marked Vietnamese speech without any failing status code — and it's not attributable to the *prompter's errors*. **Severity: Medium (brand damage, silent).**
- **Cost/latency explosion in agent loops.** Documented: a multi-agent clarification loop ran 11 days and grew API spend **$127/week → $47,000/week**, unstopped by retry logic [source: https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-03-11-llm-api-resilience-production]; retry storms multiply volume **7–12×** for hours and pushed a "one day cost more than a month" bill [source: https://www.promptzone.com/aisha_kapoor_6e606123/retry-storms-that-made-one-llm-day-cost-a-month-596i]; agent context grows **quadratically** (4k→128k tokens in 5 doubling steps) [source: https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/rate-limiting-ai-agents-preventing-llm-api-exhaustion]. Gauged against the current deterministic planner's ~0.1–0.5 s/request (lane 06), a self-correcting LLM loop is a *latency and cost* error mode, not just a quality one. **Severity: High.**

**Which LLM-layer mitigations are proven vs unsolved.** Proven: grounding via real tool/RAG over a real POI graph (drops fabricated names/hours), strict structured output (fixes syntax only), and a deterministic external validator that gates output — TripScore and Iti-Validator both show near-total elimination of *detectable* inconsistency via rule-based post-verifiers [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24719] [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09011v3]. Unproven/remains-unsolved: LLM *self*-verification (literature consensus: unreliable without external feedback — see lane 03 §4, Huang et al. ICLR'24), semantic drift inside valid JSON, priority/precedence discipline at n>8, and stateless determinism. **The unsolved set is precisely the product's correctness contract.**

## 5. Hybrid (LLM → solver) failure modes

The hybrid is the strongest *positive* lane (01/03/06 all converge on "LLM translates intent → solver schedules → LLM narrates"), but its failure surface is under-appreciated:

- **NL→structured translation loss (lost requirements).** Formalization studies show the translation degrades as language gets natural and as constraint count grows: "On the Limit of LLMs as Planning Formalizers" finds performance falls as descriptions become **more natural-sounding**, with detailed error analysis identifying *precondition/effect consistency and reachability* as the failing categories [source: https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.242/] (peer-reviewed). CaStL: **as the number of constraints increases, LLMs struggle to capture all of them**, and constraints expressed in NL routinely omit indirect objects or map one sentence to many formal statements [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22225]. NL-PDDL-Bench: LLM-generated specs "appear syntactically well-formed yet fail under strict precondition, effect-consistency, and reachability constraints" — missing object declarations, inconsistent predicates [source: https://openreview.net/pdf/fe6be201da78add2b719c932501a20c608929c9f.pdf]. Chinese/TravelPlanner migration (ChinaTravel) is the strongest *operational* proof: the SMT pipeline that sailed TravelPlanner at 91.7% **collapsed to 1.29% on human-style, under-specified queries** [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13682v5]. **Severity: Blocker.** "Meal on the way at noon" is not a constraint until someone turns it into one; if the parser drops it, the solver *certifies* a plan missing the user's stated meal — silent constraint loss.
- **"LLM picks, solver schedules" gamble — the LLM picks bad places.** The solver can only schedule the pick-set; if the LLM selector chooses: (a) places the catalogue says are closed/never-existed (param' memory, §4), (b) places whose category is mislabeled (TripTailor's documented "restaurant listed as an attraction," TripScore's mismatched IDs [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09011v3]), or (c) a set jointly infeasible (all must-haves at 09:00 + a lunch + a 16:00 closing + 40 km apart), the solver returns `INFEASIBLE`/`UNKNOWN` and the whole pipeline must either drop requirements (silent) or iterate (cost). This is the *feasibility interplay* formally demonstrated by TripScore's rule layer over real POI data. **Severity: High.**
- **Iterative repair loops that don't terminate (or blow the budget).** Planning-community evidence: LLM self-refinement on the *same* model compounds errors — FixMyPlan explicitly reverts to the original spec after failed repairs because "the compounding issues that previous research has shown can arise when LLMs attempt continuous self-refinement" [source: https://plan-fm.github.io/2025/Paper_fixmyplan.pdf] (peer-reviewed/AAAI workshop); planner-in-the-loop work converges on **"repair until the budget is exhausted"** as a *termination condition*, not success [source: https://openreview.net/pdf/fe6be201da78add2b719c932501a20c608929c9f.pdf]. Translate to product terms: every repair round is one more LLM call with quadratic-ish context, i.e., the exact failure shape that produced $47k/week loops [source: https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-03-11-llm-api-resilience-production]. **Severity: High (without a hard max-iterations + cost breaker).**
- **Grounding races: the LLM proposes, the world changes.** Even a ground-then-pick hybrid schedules against yesterday's hours/closure status. Nothing in the LLM knows today's closure; the *verification* must be a live trip to the data, not a second LLM read. **Severity: Medium.**
- **Surprising-but-correct plans are perceived as wrong.** A solver-optimal plan (lunch at 11:00 because the math says so) can contradict the Vietnamese dining norm the user expects — the solver is *right* under the user's stated constraints and *wrong* under the user's implied culture; the LLM narrating layer then paper over the mismatch or the user rejects the plan. This is the hybrid-specific face of the reward-model problem (§3). **Severity: Medium.**

## 6. ML / RL / learned-planner failure modes

Learned planners (NCO for routing, RL/LLM-RL agents for itinerary) are the newest candidate. The failure evidence is consistent and discouraging for a correctness-critical, small-data product:

- **Reward hacking is the default, not the edge case.** In recommender/RL literature: PPO/DPO "can attain high reward-model scores while substantially degrading recommendation quality … exploiting weaknesses in the learned reward" (Exp-RSFT analysis, peer-reviewed preprint), with empirically inverted-U reward-vs-quality curves once temperature is tuned below optimum — i.e., *the standard pipeline over-optimizes an imperfect surrogate* [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00816] [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10279]. DeepTravel (the closest thing to a production RL travel-planner, DiDi-deployed, 82% online itinerary accuracy) lists **reward hacking as a monitored failure mode** requiring explicit diagnostics ("Very low sample keep rate … signals data regime misalignment; unusually short responses and few turns often signal reward hacking") [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21842v2]. If a Deployed RL system must *instrument* for reward hacking, a solo-dev bespoke learned planner has no defense. **Severity: High.**
- **Generalization gaps across cities/languages.** SFT over-adapts to dense users — "SFT models tend to overfit users with rich interaction histories, leading to weak generalization in cold-start scenarios"; the same paper's remedy is prompt-policy over knowledge graphs, *not* a learned router [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08012]. Travel recommendation's own literature stresses "**exceedingly sparse and a large number of cold-start users**" as inherent [source: https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3570959] (peer-reviewed). For a Hanoi-first product expanding to new cities, a learned model trained on Hanoi POIs transfers poorly — including the hours/churn statistics that are the domain's core failure (§1). **Severity: High.**
- **Offline-evaluation ceiling hides the failure until deploy.** A rigorous DQN study found the tuned RL policy **statistically tied a simple static heuristic**, while behaving completely differently — "the simulation environment creates a performance ceiling inadequate to distinguish a good policy from a potentially great one" [source: https://openreview.net/forum?id=KVQJpmCYDn] (peer-reviewed/OpenReview). Learned planners look successful in sim and degrade on real constraints — exactly where the "never infeasible" contract lives. **Severity: Medium-High.**
- **Unverifiable learned outputs are a correctness dead-end.** A neural router produces *a schedule*, with no proof; verifying requires re-running a deterministic checker (destroying the point) or trusting the model. NCO/routing literature at THIS scale (n≤40) shows exact/heuristic solvers match or beat learned methods (lane 06); correctness-critical production is not where learned planners win today [source: 06-honest-baseline.md §6 referencing NCO-vs-exact at n≤10, moderate]. **Severity: Medium-High.**
- **"Learning from user feedback" compounds the data problems, it doesn't fix them.** The models learn from the same gamed reviews and stale-closure labels (§1). A policy optimized to "what users clicked" absorbs manipulation (click/review fraud) as ground truth. **Severity: Medium.**

**Mitigation honesty:** the only *positive* production learnings are (a) DiDi's *hierarchical deterministic verifier* used as the RL reward signal — i.e., feasibility checked by rules, not by the model [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21842v2], and (b) treating LLM/RL as *proposers* inside a guarded loop, never as the source of truth. Both re-affirm the deterministic-gate pattern: learned components can accelerate the front of the pipeline (parsing/selection/copy) but must not own feasibility.

## 7. Operational reliability failure modes (common to all architectures)

- **Provider availability is the weak link.** LLM API providers run at ~99.0–99.5% uptime vs ~99.97% for big-cloud (≈3.5 days/yr vs ~2.5 h/yr downtime; industry average *fell* from 99.66%→99.46% between Q1'24 and Q1'25), and AI/ML APIs rank *last* among 215+ tracked API services; OpenAI logged **11 incidents in January 2026 (one/2.5 days)**, Anthropic had multi-per-week incidents incl. a **30-hour resolution cycle** [source: https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-03-11-llm-api-resilience-production] [source: https://www.tierzero.ai/blog/ai-provider-outages-2026/] (practitioner write-ups — magnitude credible, treated as indicative). A single-provider product has no plan; the current pipeline already has a deterministic fallback (`AI_FALLBACK_NOTE`), which is the correct posture. **Severity: High for LLM-dependent variants; Note-for-the-planner (which is local-fast).**
- **Silent quality degradation (the 200-OK trap).** The August 2025 bug set shows providers can serve wrong-language/wrong-logic output under **normal HTTP** for weeks — undetectable via status codes, discovered via user complaints [source: https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-03-11-llm-api-resilience-production]. All "validate the LLM" designs (schema check, token counts) pass while semantics rot. **Severity: High.**
- **Retry storms & cost spikes are a *design* failure, not just provider failure.** Retry-without-jitter compounded 3⁵=243 calls per request at the service layer; the "$47k/week loop"; context-quadratic runaway in agent loops — all preventable with budget caps, jittered backoff, circuit breakers, and cost-velocity breakers [source: https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-03-11-llm-api-resilience-production] [source: https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/rate-limiting-ai-agents-preventing-llm-api-exhaustion] [source: https://www.promptzone.com/aisha_kapoor_6e606123/retry-storms-that-made-one-llm-day-cost-a-month-596i]. **Severity: High if any LLM loop exists without a breaker.**
- **Routing / driving-time matrix degradation.** Static OSM-speed travel-time estimation errors of −40% to +25% vs reality [source: https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLVIII-4-W7-2023/109/2023/isprs-archives-XLVIII-4-W7-2023-109-2023.pdf] mean "impossible travel time" is *common* if a static matrix is trusted as exact; even real-time estimation has 85% of runs <20% error and badly degrades during incidents/sparse detector coverage [source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3141/2049-03]. Time-dependence matters: afternoon-peak skims underestimate by ~6.5-min RMSE in one Munich study [source: https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1585399/1585399.pdf]. A 20-minute buffer is not optional margin — it is a load-bearing assumption for the "impossible travel time" guarantee. **Severity: Medium-High for all architectures.**
- **Weather forecast skill is a hard ceiling, not a bug.** Peer-reviewed: short-range high-precipitation warnings "fall short of common skill requirements"; optimal-threshold moderate-heavy events get ~55% hit/pF ~0.55, and sub-20-km/short-timescale intense rain is minimally predictable — "users … should not base their decisions on NWP" and should use nowcasting [source: https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/24/2793/2024/]. A planner that reorders indoor/outdoor on a 3-day rain forecast from Open-Meteo is making decisions on data with large error bars; it should treat *weather as a soft prior + explicit "plan may change" UI*, not a hard constraint (the product already ships "Forecasts may change" copy — `planner.py`). **Severity: Medium.**
- **Caching and determinism interact badly with non-deterministic LLMs.** Agentic caching breaks because LLM calls are non-deterministic (AI21's lab: "the cache is broken!!" — the same agent re-run does not reproduce) — cache keys must encode pipeline position, not just prompt [source: https://www.ai21.com/blog/caching-in-agentic-llm-pipelines/]. And the only surviving mitigation for LLM grads is *statistical* (multi-run + variance reporting) as providers deprecate temperature/seed knobs entirely (Claude Opus 4.7/4.8 reject temperature in [0,1)) [source: https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26185v1]. **Severity: Medium.**
- **Streaming/abort races & multilingual escapes.** Streaming partial plans get cached/aborted inconsistently (lane 06 documents the SSE path); the provider-side wrong-language bug shows locale fidelity is provider-controlled, not prompt-controlled [source: https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-03-11-llm-api-resilience-production]. **Severity: Medium.**

## 8. Constraints NONE of the architectures can cleanly represent

These are not "fixable in a rewrite"; they are structural:

1. **Today's special hours / temporary closure** (museums closed Mondays, Tết closures, road closures). No architecture — greedy, solver, LLM, or hybrid — *knows* tomorrow's exception unless the data layer has ```specialDays```-grade freshness (Google Places has it in the API but it must be *harvested and cached with a TTL*) [source: https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/reference/rest/v1/places]. **Blocker** for the "never infeasible" contract unless imported as data.
2. **Meal precedence with human-rhythm flexibility.** Precedence (breakfast→lunch→dinner) is easy; *"lunch must feel like lunch (Korean diners, real Vietnamese 11:30–13:30 social table)"* is a cultural prior no constraint language captures without an explicit reward term — and the term is exactly the reward-model problem (§3).
3. **"Vibes" and safety intangibles** (walkability at 22:00, family-friendliness, air quality, seasonal crowd). Never representable as hard constraints.
4. **Trustworthy ratings** — §1.3 shows the input signal is adversarially manipulable; any objective built on it inherits manipulation.
5. **Multi-language correctness of a narrative about a live schedule** — translation quality, wrong-language incidents, and "words that sound great over an infeasible schedule."
6. **Real-time travel variance** — the drive time is a distribution, and all three architectures treat it as a scalar.

The product can be honest about the *ceiling*: represent what's representable, and keep a "check before you go / plans can change" UX for the rest.

## 10. Executive summary (~250 words)

Every candidate architecture fails in this domain, and they fail in overlapping but distinct ways, dominated by bad input data rather than weak algorithms. **Data: Blocker.** OSM `opening_hours` are missing or malformed for a large share of POIs (~quarter have problems, Vespucci; sub-22% completeness for whole POI classes in ground-truth studies), restaurants churn 20–30% a year, closures linger in catalogues for weeks (even Google's own spikes/status lag reality), two major catalogue providers disagree about hours on ~76% of tested restaurants, and ~16% of reviews on one platform are machine-flagged as fake. No scheduler — heuristic, CP-SAT, LLM, hybrid, or learned — schedules a closed door away; only data plumbing (verified whitelists, closure-velocity signals, user correction) fixes it. **LLM: Blocker.** Benchmark and guardrail work shows designed-for-scheduling LLMs fail constraints (hallucinated POIs/flights; hours+chronology the most-broken constraint class), silent reordering breaks meal precedence, temperature-0 is not deterministic (up to 15% accuracy variation across identical runs), and agent loops are dollar/latency bombs without hard breakers. **Solver: High.** Correctness is provable only for the encoding: `UNKNOWN` under time-limit, over-constrained real-world inputs, and the reward-model problem ("optimal but nonsense for humans") are intrinsic. **Hybrid:** best *prospects* but only failures are new: NL→solver constraint loss is documented to collapse to ~1% on natural queries (ChinaTravel), "LLM picks, solver schedules" manufactures infeasible pick-sets, and repair loops need termination and cost guarantees. **Learned:** reward hacking, cold-start and cross-city generalization gaps, and offline-eval ceilings make them unviable as the correctness owner today. **Mitigations are all deterministic:** an independent validator gate, relax-then-tighten, fallback tiers, budget breakers, and data-freshness machinery — which the current pipeline already half-contains. The product's "never infeasible" guarantee is achievable *only* by a deterministic, validator-gated core fed by verified data; the LLM's role is intent-translation, selection, and narration — never feasibility.

## 11. Top 5 strong findings (severity-tagged)

1. **[Blocker] The dominant failure mode is data, not algorithm — and it affects every architecture uniformly.** OSM hours malformed/missing at scale, POI classes down to ~22% completeness, restaurants churning 20–30%/yr, closures lagging catalogues by weeks, and hours disagreeing across authorities (~76% of a tested restaurant sample mismatch Google vs Apple). No scheduler can guarantee feasibility against these facts; only verified-data plumbing can. [TRB POI completeness; Vespucci docs; JOG Ottawa Google-Places closures; Mobal Finnish hours study]
2. **[Blocker] Pure-LLM scheduling fails the product's correctness contract on every axis we can test: entity hallucination (incl. non-existent flights/POIs), hours+chronology as the most-broken constraint class, silent precedence-violating "valid JSON", and non-determinism up to 15% at temperature 0.** Structured output and grounding fix the envelope, not the content; no self-verification scheme is trustworthy. [TripTailor; TripScore; Iti-Validator; PlanBench/Mystery; Atıl et al. determinism audit]
3. **[High] All LLM-using variants (pure, hybrid, RL) carry cost/latency failure modes that are *design* failures: 11-day $47k/week loops, 7–12× retry storms, quadratic agent context, and provider "200-OK wrong-language/wrong-logic" degradation.** Without circuit breakers, budgets, and fallback tiers, these become availability incidents. [tianpan resilience write-up; TrueFoundry gateway; PromptZone postmortem; Nordic APIs/tierzero incidents]
4. **[High] The solver family trades heuristic sloppiness for three distinct failure classes: `UNKNOWN` under time limits, over-constrained real-world inputs (with the relaxation contract unspecified by the solver), and the reward-model problem — the solver certifies optimality for an encoding that omits everything users actually value (ratings manipulability, culture, crowd, vibes), and human evals show "feasible" ≠ "good".** Determinism also requires enforced discipline (single-worker, pinned seed/version; documented INFEASIBLE-vs-OPTIMAL flips). [CP-SAT status docs; StackOverflow flakiness report; TripTailor human eval; d-krupke primer]
5. **[High] The hybrid's specific failure — NL→formal constraint loss — is the sharpest known version of the class: ChinaTravel shows a solver pipeline engineered to sail a benchmark at 91.7% collapsing to ~1.3% on natural human-style queries, and formalization studies show constraint capture degrades as n and naturalness rise.** The "LLM picks, solver schedules" gamble ships infeasible pick-sets and requires termination/cost guarantees on repair loops. [ChinaTravel; Huang & Zhang ACL 2025; CaStL; NL-PDDL-Bench; FixMyPlan]

## 12. Confidence and ground-truth tally

**Confidence: 8/10.** The failure-mode inventory is unusually well-evidenced: the core claims (LLM feasibility collapse on objective benchmarks, hours+chronology as dominant violation class, temperature-0 non-determinism, provider outage/retry-storm economics, POI incompleteness and closure lag, review fraud prevalence) each rest on ≥2 independent sources, most peer-reviewed or primary. Confidence is not 9–10 because: (a) a nontrivial share of the operational evidence is practitioner/vendor blogs (tianpan, tierzero, mobal, truefoundry, promptzone, financialcontent) whose magnitudes I treat as indicative but whose exact numbers are single-source; (b) several numbers are dated (OSM parser stats, Yelp 16%, 2017 local-listing 43/41%) yet the *direction* of each is stable across sources; (c) I could not independently estimate the *base rate* of wrong/closed POIs in the actual Hanoi catalogue — the domain applies the global evidence, but the magnitude here is first-party-measurable, not measured; (d) no user-A/B exists for any architecture on this product, so human-perceived severity tiers are reasoned extrapolations.

**Ground-truth tally:**
- Externally corroborated (≥2 sources, peer-reviewed or primary): 23 claims — OSM hours ~quarter problematic/<10% uncorrectable + parser 99.1%; POI completeness 22–73% + OSM overcount due to closures; Google-Places closure lag + 71%/44% confirmed openings; restaurant POI churn 20–30%/yr; 76% cross-catalogue hours mismatch (single marketing study, weaker); Yelp 16% fake-filter + fraud incentives; Google 170M reviews/12M profiles removed (primary); CP-SAT status enum + integer-only + UNKNOWN semantics; INFEASIBLE-vs-OPTIMAL flakiness anecdote; TravelPlanner 0.6% / ChinaTravel 0–2.6% / Mystery-Blocksworld ~53%; TripTailor within-sandbox 78% vs 96.6% + <10% human-level + 17km vs 7.3km; TripScore hours+chronology dominant violations; Iti-Validator non-existent flights + rule-validator 100% repair; temperature-0 non-determinism 15% (peer) + 80-unique-completions (vendor lab) + BF16 precision variance (NeurIPS'25); NL→PDDL degradation with naturalness + constraint-count (ACL'25, CaStL, NL-PDDL-Bench); FixMyPlan self-refinement compounding; reward hacking in RLHF-rec (2 preprints) + DeepTravel reward-hacking monitoring; DQN-ties-heuristic offline-learning ceiling; short-range precipitation forecast skill ceiling (NHESS'24); OSM-speed travel error −40%/+25% (ISPRS'23); LLM API uptime ~99.0–99.5% + incidents (2 practitioner sources).
- Model judgment / single-source flagged as estimate: exact $47k/week and 76% magnitudes; the severity tiering itself (Blocker/High/…) for this product; the transfer of German/OSM completeness numbers to Vietnam; the claim that the current validator's precedence bug is the cheapest win (first-party lane 06 measured it — first-party).
